# Cranioplasty biomaterial selection

Scope: evidence summary for comparing PEEK, 3D-printed titanium (Ti-6Al-4V), PMMA and
autologous bone. Findings come from abstracts and summaries gathered in a literature search;
pooled estimates were **not re-verified against full texts**. This is decision support for a
planning conversation, not a recommendation for an individual patient.

## PEEK
Keywords: PEEK, polyetheretherketone, polymer, radiolucent, patient-specific, PSI
Evidence: literature (abstract level)

Pre-fabricated patient-specific polymer. Pooled evidence shows infection rates similar to
titanium, with lower exposure and overall complication rates than titanium in one meta-analysis
and the lowest reoperation risk in a network meta-analysis; direct randomised evidence is
pending (RCT protocol, 140 patients) [1][2][4 in matrix]. Radiolucent with minimal imaging
artifact, which helps postoperative tumour or haemorrhage surveillance. Thermal conductivity
is close to bone. Does not osseointegrate. Cost and lead time are higher than mesh or PMMA.

## Titanium (Ti-6Al-4V)
Keywords: titanium, Ti-6Al-4V, mesh, 3D-printed titanium, metal, conductivity, artifact, exposure
Evidence: literature (abstract level)

Strong, available as patient-specific printed plates or intra-operative mesh. Infection rates
are similar to PEEK, but implant exposure was higher than PEEK in one meta-analysis [1 in matrix].
High thermal conductivity can cause scalp temperature sensitivity/paresthesia [4 in matrix].
Produces CT streak artifact. **Thin or irradiated scalp:** the reviewed sources do not quantify
exposure risk by material in irradiated fields; one RCT protocol excluded prior radiotherapy
altogether [4 in matrix]. Treat thin/irradiated scalp as a surgeon-review flag rather than a
settled contraindication.

## PMMA
Keywords: PMMA, polymethylmethacrylate, bone cement, acrylic, in situ, exothermic, thermal injury, moulded
Evidence: literature

PMMA polymerisation is exothermic. A bench study measured temperatures above 81.4 °C (180 °F);
cold-saline irrigation limited heat, but control was partially lost at 7 mm thickness and the
authors concluded underlying thermal injury is inevitable with thicker implants [1]. A cadaver
model found temperature rising with PMMA thickness and nearby tissue exposed to > 50 °C for
prolonged periods, and recommended pre-fabricated PMMA prostheses [2]. When moulded in situ,
protect dura and brain from heat.

Sources:
1. Bench study of PMMA polymerisation temperature (Springer, BF01808863). https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF01808863.pdf
2. Temperature elevation during simulated PMMA cranioplasty in a cadaver model. https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/temperature-elevation-during-simulated-polymethylmethacrylate-pmm-3/

## Autologous bone and resorption
Keywords: autologous, autograft, bone flap, resorption, pediatric, children, cryopreserved, storage, timing
Evidence: literature

Bone flap resorption is the main failure mode. Pediatric resorption rates are reported between
20% and 50% [1]. A multicentre study of 359 children found age at cranioplasty was the strongest
predictor of resorption (risk falling about 1% per month of age), and 21.7% of 240 bone-graft
patients needed repeat surgery for resorption; external ventricular drains and lumbar shunts
were additional risk factors [2]. Early cranioplasty (within six weeks of craniectomy) has been
associated with lower resorption [1]. A 2013 review suggested cryopreserved flaps may resorb
more than room-temperature-stored flaps, and fresh autograft less than stored autograft [3].
Evidence conflicts on the effect of defect size and age in some reviews [3].

Sources:
1. Pediatric cranioplasty failure meta-analysis (2025). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12356755/
2. Multicentre pediatric cranioplasty study, 359 children (J Neurosurg Pediatr 2018), as reported. https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s00381-018-4025-1
3. 2013 systematic review of pediatric cranioplasty outcomes (11 studies). https://read.qxmd.com/read/23790219/outcomes-of-cranioplasty-following-decompressive-craniectomy-in-the-pediatric-population
